# Stakeholder Interview Guide

**Purpose:** Run a structured 60-90 minute discovery interview.
**Use when:** Discovery step 2. Run separate sessions for stakeholders with different roles.

## Before the call

- Read the intake form and review their current site and channels.
- Prepare three specific observations to test, labeled [Hypothesis].
- Confirm recording consent in writing.

## Question set

Ask open questions. Follow up with "Can you give me a recent example?"

**Business (15 min)**
1. What does the business sell, and to whom?
2. Which product or service matters most for revenue in the next 12 months?
3. How do customers find you today? How do you know?
4. What changed that made this project necessary now?

**Users (15 min)**
5. Describe your best customer. What did they need when they first contacted you?
6. What questions do customers ask most before buying?
7. Where do people drop off or complain?

**Brand and competition (15 min)**
8. Name three competitors. What do they do better than you? Worse?
9. Which three words should people use about you? Which words must they never use?
10. Show me a website or product you admire outside your industry. What do you like about it?

**Project (15 min)**
11. What must this project achieve for you to call it a success in six months?
12. What is out of scope?
13. Who approves designs and the final launch? How long do approvals usually take?
14. Who will write copy and supply photos? By when?
15. What systems must this connect to? (payment, CRM, inventory, email)
16. Which languages must the product support at launch, and later?

**Constraints (10 min)**
17. What budget range have you planned?
18. Is the deadline fixed by an event, or flexible?
19. Any legal, regulatory, or data requirements we should know?

## After the call

- Write notes within 24 hours using evidence labels.
- Send open questions in one message, not five.

## Common mistakes

- Pitching during discovery. Listen first.
- Asking leading questions ("Wouldn't a modern design help?").
