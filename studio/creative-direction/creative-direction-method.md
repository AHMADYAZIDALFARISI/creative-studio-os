# Creative Direction Method

**Purpose:** Produce a documented creative direction that ties visual choices to business context.
**Use when:** After positioning is approved and before any screen design or Stitch exploration.
**Inputs:** Discovery summary, positioning, competitor findings.
**Output:** A completed [templates/creative-brief.md](../../templates/creative-brief.md) with two or three directions, then one approved direction.

## The ten parts of a direction

1. **Strategic rationale:** the business goal this direction serves.
2. **Audience insight:** one observation about the audience that shapes the look.
3. **Visual concept:** a two-sentence idea. Example: "A working field notebook. Information feels gathered by hand, measured, and dated."
4. **Typography direction:** families, scale contrast, and why.
5. **Color direction:** roles, proportions, and why.
6. **Layout principles:** grid, density, alignment, use of whitespace.
7. **Image and asset direction:** what to shoot or source, what to avoid.
8. **Interaction and motion direction:** what moves, why, and the timing range.
9. **Do's and don'ts:** five each, concrete.
10. **Reference evaluation:** what each reference teaches, using [reference-evaluation.md](../references/reference-evaluation.md).

## Making directions genuinely different

Vary at least two of these axes between directions:

- Density: sparse vs information-rich.
- Typographic voice: editorial serif, grotesk, mono, display.
- Structure: strict grid vs broken grid vs list-led.
- Image role: photo-led, illustration-led, type-led, data-led.
- Tone: calm, energetic, technical, warm.

## Presenting to the client

- Show each direction on the same two screens (for example home hero and a product detail) so the client compares ideas, not content.
- Explain each with the rationale first, visuals second.
- Recommend one, with a reason.
- Ask the client to choose a direction, not to merge three.

## Quality check

- Every direction passes the [anti-slop audit](anti-slop-audit.md) at "concept" level.
- A teammate can predict how a new page would look from the brief alone.
